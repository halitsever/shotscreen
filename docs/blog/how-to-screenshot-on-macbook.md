# Taking a screenshot on a MacBook, step by step

<p class="post-meta">By <a href="https://halit.org">Halit Sever</a> · <time datetime="2026-11-28">28 November 2026</time></p>

<p class="lead">Every MacBook, Air or Pro, takes screenshots the same way: hold Shift and Command and press 3, 4 or 5. Here is each one as a step-by-step, with the details that matter on a laptop, such as selecting with the trackpad and the Touch Bar on older MacBook Pro models.</p>

## The whole screen

Press <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>3</kbd> together and let go. You hear a camera sound if the volume is up, a thumbnail appears in the bottom-right corner, and a few seconds later a PNG file named "Screenshot" with the date and time lands on the desktop.

The macOS steps here were checked against Apple's [macOS User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/take-screenshots-or-screen-recordings-mh26782/mac) for macOS 27 Golden Gate and [Apple's screenshot guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102646) in November 2026. They have worked the same way since macOS Mojave unless noted.

## Part of the screen, with the trackpad

1. Press <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>4</kbd>. The pointer turns into a crosshair.
2. Press and hold on the trackpad, then drag over the area.
3. Let go to take the screenshot.

Hold <kbd>Space</kbd> while dragging to move the selection, <kbd>Shift</kbd> to fix one side, or <kbd>Option</kbd> to resize from the centre. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to cancel.

## One window

Press <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>4</kbd>, then <kbd>Space</kbd>. The pointer becomes a camera and the window under it is highlighted. Tap the trackpad to capture that window. Hold <kbd>Option</kbd> as you click to leave out the window's shadow.

## Every option: the Screenshot app

<kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>5</kbd> opens a toolbar at the bottom of the screen with buttons for the whole screen, a window or a selected portion, and an **Options** menu for the timer, the save location and the floating thumbnail. It also records the screen; for that, see Avora's guide to [screen recording on a Mac](https://projects.halit.org/avora-screen-recorder/blog/how-to-screen-record-on-mac).

## MacBook Pro with a Touch Bar

On MacBook Pro models with a Touch Bar, <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>6</kbd> saves a picture of the Touch Bar itself. You can also add a Screenshot button to the Touch Bar's Control Strip in the keyboard settings.

## Screenshots straight to the clipboard

Add <kbd>Control</kbd> to any of the shortcuts, for example <kbd>Control</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>4</kbd>, and the screenshot is copied instead of saved. Paste it with <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd>. The details are in [copy and paste a screenshot on Mac](/blog/copy-paste-screenshot-mac).

## On an external display

When the MacBook is connected to a monitor, <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>4</kbd> works on any screen: drag the selection on the display you want. The full keyboard list, including how to change the keys, is in [Mac screenshot shortcuts](/blog/mac-screenshot-shortcuts).

## Website screenshots on a MacBook

A MacBook Air's screen is not wide, so a browser screenshot often includes the tab bar and a cut-off page. [Shotscreen Browser](/) renders the page at a fixed size, including Desktop (1440 x 900) and Ultrawide (1920 x 1080), scaled down on screen to fit the window, and captures the page at that viewport size when you press <kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd>.

## FAQ

### How do I screenshot on a MacBook Air?

Press Shift-Command-3 for the whole screen or Shift-Command-4 and drag for part of it. The file is saved to the desktop.

### Is it the same on a MacBook Pro?

Yes. All MacBook models use the same shortcuts. Models with a Touch Bar also have Shift-Command-6 to capture the Touch Bar.

### How do I screenshot on a MacBook without the keyboard?

Open the Screenshot app from Spotlight or the Utilities folder in Applications and click a capture button in its toolbar.
